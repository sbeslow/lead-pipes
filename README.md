# Lead Pipes

Open source projects focused on lead in Chicago's drinking water infrastructure.

I think we can all remember the tragedy of what happened in Flint, Michigan a few years ago. But the truth is, Chicago has more lead water lines than any city in America — over 400,000 — more than twice number 2, Cleveland. The US EPA says no amount of lead is safe. It damages kids' brains, causes miscarriages, and increases heart disease in adults. In 2024, the EPA gave most municipalities 10 years to replace their lead pipes, but given the magnitude of the problem, Chicago was given 20 years. But the city says it will take 50 years, which is a ridiculous amount of time to let a public health hazard linger on drinking water, a basic human right. But if you look at the city's progress over the past 5 years, the existing pace will take 147 years. I don't know what the solution to the problem is, but I do believe it is more solvable than the city's current momentum. There are hundreds of millions of dollars of federal money that is earmarked for this that have been unused. By enacting some common sense policies and strategies, Newark was able to replace all of their lines in 3 years at a cost of $8000 per line, whereas in Chicago, it supposedly costs $35,000.

This repo is for exploring data, telling the story, and finding ways to solve this problem with more urgency.

## Projects

- [`park-district-fountains/`](park-district-fountains/) — FOIA'd lead testing data for all drinking water fountains in Chicago parks (2,807 fixtures, 402 parks, 5 years of test results). Live at [shouldidrinkhere.com](https://shouldidrinkhere.com).
